## Executive briefing

1. **EQT / Cleanaway remains the defining Australian transaction.** EQT Infrastructure has proposed acquiring Cleanaway at A$3.13/share, implying approximately A$9.4bn enterprise value and a 32.1% premium. Cleanaway has granted up to nine weeks of exclusive due diligence and currently intends to recommend the proposal. Reuters notes the valuation is about 10x expected 2026 EBITDA, making operational improvement and growth central to the investment case.
2. **The Cleanaway thesis is operational, not merely financial.** EQT's global environmental-services experience, combined with Cleanaway's national network, creates a potential value-creation agenda around asset utilisation, fleet/network productivity, procurement, digitisation, data and operating-model improvement.
3. **Hanwha/Austal remains the strongest geopolitical M&A case.** Hanwha is pursuing Austal's US operations for US$1.05–1.2bn after a prior attempt to acquire the whole company failed amid regulatory concerns. Austal's board has granted due-diligence access. The case illustrates how defence priorities and geopolitics can alter cross-border deal feasibility.
4. **FleetPartners is a live competitive auction.** Orix has proposed A$3.80/share, while PEP has raised its proposal to A$4.00/share; Element remains involved and Smartgroup has reportedly considered an offer. The possibility of combining FleetPartners with SG Fleet adds a competition and portfolio-consolidation dimension.
5. **The regulatory environment is now part of transaction strategy.** Australia's mandatory merger-control regime has applied since 1 January 2026. The ACCC's current guidance makes notification, suspensory approval, statutory timing and information-gathering powers material considerations for deal planning.

## Corporate M&A

The current market signal is increasingly about portfolio reshaping and strategic capability acquisition rather than simple scale consolidation. Defence, essential services, healthcare, mobility and infrastructure assets are attracting capital because of scarcity, recurring demand, strategic positioning and identifiable operational levers.

## PE

The strongest PE theme is operational transformation. Cleanaway represents infrastructure/essential services plus operational improvement; FleetPartners represents consolidation and mobility; stressed healthcare situations create separation and turnaround opportunities. The better origination question is which sponsors have capital plus a credible operational thesis, rather than simply which sponsors are active.

## Infrastructure / institutional / sovereign capital

Cleanaway is the clearest current example. Waste management has recurring demand, physical networks, processing assets, route density and environmental-transition optionality. Institutional capital can underwrite the asset as an essential-service infrastructure platform while pursuing operational improvement.

## Cross-border

Two transactions frame the market: EQT acquiring an Australian essential-services platform and Hanwha pursuing a US defence capability carved from an Australian company. Both demonstrate that Australian assets are increasingly being valued for strategic capability and infrastructure position as well as EBITDA.

## ACCC / FIRB / regulation

The ACCC's merger-process guidelines state that from 1 January 2026 Australia operates a mandatory merger-control regime. Businesses meeting the relevant thresholds must notify and cannot complete notified acquisitions without approval. The ACCC also has section 155 compulsory information-gathering powers and can use them during Phase 1, Phase 2 or public-benefit assessment. A 12-month limit applies once an acquisition is cleared before a notification becomes stale.

**AD implication:** regulatory strategy should be designed alongside the transaction thesis, particularly for contested auctions, serial acquisitions and sponsor roll-ups.

## Deloitte AD lens

1. The value-creation bridge matters more than the headline acquisition price in infrastructure deals.
2. Carve-outs are becoming strategic value-creation programmes rather than back-office separation exercises.
3. Competitive auctions require commercial, operational and regulatory thinking together.
4. The strongest PE origination targets have operational dislocation that can be converted into a measurable transformation thesis.
5. Technology should be positioned as an enabler of value realisation, not forced into every transaction narrative.

## My right-to-play

The distinctive bridge is Technology DD → Separation/TSA → Target Operating Model → Enterprise Architecture → Cloud/Data Transformation → Value Realisation.
