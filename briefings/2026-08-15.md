# Deloitte Australia M&A Intelligence — 15 August 2026

## Executive briefing

- **EQT / Cleanaway remains the headline Australian transaction.** EQT Infrastructure's proposed acquisition values Cleanaway at approximately **A$9.4bn enterprise value / A$7bn equity value**, at A$3.13 per share and a 32.1% premium. Cleanaway has granted exclusive due diligence for up to nine weeks and currently intends to recommend the offer. Reuters notes the price is around 10x expected 2026 EBITDA, making operational value creation central to the investment thesis. Source: Reuters, 13 Aug 2026 — https://www.reuters.com/commentary/breakingviews/eqt-unearths-7-bln-aussie-trash-to-cash-deal-2026-08-13/

- **Cleanaway is also becoming a contested shareholder story.** Some investors argue the company may be selling before the benefits of recent investments are fully realised; Challenger and UniSuper together hold around 16%, making shareholder support important to the outcome. Source: The Australian, 14 Aug 2026.

- **FleetPartners has developed into a three-way takeover contest.** Element has proposed A$4.00/share, while PEP and Orix have competing proposals. The asset is strategically attractive because of its novated-leasing exposure and EV-related tax incentives. Source: The Australian DataRoom, 11 Aug 2026.

- **Healthscope remains a stressed, execution-heavy situation.** A Calvary-led consortium has increased its proposal, but unresolved issues have delayed a decision. The proposed structure divides hospital assets among several operators, making separation, transition and continuity of operations central to value. Source: The Australian DataRoom, 14 Aug 2026.

- **Inghams is the key PE watchlist name.** TPG, BGH, PEP, Paine Schwartz and Roc Partners have reportedly shown interest, although Inghams says it has not received a formal offer. FY26 results on 21 August are the next major catalyst. Source: The Australian DataRoom, 14 Aug 2026.

## Major transactions / live situations

### EQT → Cleanaway

**A$3.13/share | ~A$7bn equity | ~A$9.4bn EV**

The central deal question is not simply whether EQT can acquire Cleanaway, but whether the sponsor can generate sufficient EBITDA growth and operational improvement to support a roughly 10x entry multiple. Relevant value levers include network optimisation, fleet productivity, asset utilisation, procurement, digitisation, data/analytics and technology-enabled field operations.

### FleetPartners

The competitive process now has PEP, Orix and Element in the frame, with Smartgroup also reported to be exploring options. PEP's existing SG Fleet ownership adds a consolidation/synergy angle, while Mitsubishi Motors' 19.9% stake creates an additional strategic dimension.

### Healthscope

The Calvary/PEP/Acurio/KnG consortium remains the leading reported path, but unresolved issues mean the process is not complete. The transaction is unusually relevant to Deloitte because it combines stressed M&A, multi-party separation, TSA, workforce transition, landlord relationships and technology disentanglement.

## PE

The current pattern is **operational transformation rather than financial engineering alone**:

- Cleanaway — infrastructure + operational improvement
- FleetPartners — consolidation + mobility/EV platform
- Healthscope — distressed restructuring + separation
- Inghams — potential buy-and-fix / operational recovery

The Deloitte proposition should therefore move beyond "transaction diligence" toward **value-creation thesis + execution capability**.

## Infrastructure / institutional capital

Cleanaway is a strong institutional-infrastructure case: essential service demand, physical network, route density, processing assets and potential operational optimisation. EQT's global environmental-services experience strengthens the strategic rationale.

## Cross-border

Two transactions frame the market:

1. **EQT → Cleanaway:** European infrastructure capital acquiring an Australian essential-services platform.
2. **Hanwha → Austal US:** Korean strategic capital pursuing US defence capability from an Australian parent.

Together they illustrate increasing global demand for Australian platforms with strategic scarcity, infrastructure position or government-linked capability.

## ACCC / regulatory

The new Australian merger-control regime makes cumulative acquisition strategy increasingly relevant, particularly for PE roll-ups and platform strategies. The key client question is shifting from **"Will this acquisition clear?"** to **"How does our acquisition programme look through the ACCC's lens?"**

## Deloitte AD lens

**The strongest conversation today:** Cleanaway is a good example of the transaction thesis becoming an operating-model thesis. At a roughly 10x EBITDA entry point, value creation needs to be explicit before signing, not treated as a post-deal aspiration.

For an AD with a technology-transformation background, the differentiated position is:

**Transaction thesis → Technology DD → Separation / TSA → Target operating model → Data / cloud / architecture → Transformation → Value realisation.**

## My right-to-play

The strongest positioning is not pure M&A banking. It is the bridge between **deal rationale and technology-enabled operating performance**. Cleanaway, Healthscope and Austal are particularly good examples because technology, separation and operating-model execution can materially influence whether the transaction thesis is realised.
