# Deloitte Australia M&A Intelligence — 12 August 2026

## 1. Executive briefing

- **Strategic cross-border M&A remains active in defence and industrial capability.** Hanwha has offered about US$1.05–1.2bn for Austal's US operations, a carve-out that preserves Austal's Australian, Philippine and Vietnamese operations. The transaction is strategically driven by Hanwha's expansion in US defence shipbuilding rather than a simple whole-company acquisition. [S1]
- **PE competitive processes remain an important source of Australian transaction activity.** Agilex Biolabs, owned by Healius, has attracted roughly six to seven strategic and PE bidders, with reported expectations of A$150–200m and a possible September transaction. [S2]
- **PE bid certainty is highly sensitive to price and value-creation assumptions.** PEP has reportedly stepped back from oOh!media after failing to secure internal approval at the proposed price, while I Squared remains a potential bidder and has a possible cross-border combination thesis involving Stroer. [S3]
- **Digital infrastructure is becoming a multi-sector M&A/value-creation theme.** Centuria is considering converting industrial assets into data centres, with potential for more than 250MW of conversions. This links property, power, data-centre infrastructure, AI demand and capital structuring. [S4]
- **Regulation is becoming part of the digital-infrastructure investment thesis.** New Australian standards require new data centres to be majority renewable powered, creating implications for project feasibility, power procurement, approvals and infrastructure sequencing. [S5]
- **Resources remain strategically important to Australia's M&A and capital markets story.** Glencore's planned ASX secondary listing is explicitly aimed at improving access to Australia's deep institutional investor base while it pursues copper growth and retains optionality around future M&A. [S6]

## 3. Corporate M&A

The strongest corporate signal today is **portfolio and capability reshaping rather than indiscriminate expansion**. Austal is a useful example: the proposed sale isolates the US operation, which generated the majority of Austal's FY2025 pre-tax profit, while leaving the Australian operation outside the transaction. [S1]

SGH is also signalling continued M&A appetite while highlighting AI and digital-infrastructure investment opportunities. Its positioning around data-centre development illustrates how corporate M&A and capital allocation increasingly intersect with power, land and technology infrastructure. [S7]

## 4. Private equity

Healthcare and media illustrate two different PE dynamics:

- **Healthcare:** Agilex has a broad bidder field, suggesting continued sponsor appetite for specialised healthcare assets with international growth and platform characteristics. [S2]
- **Media:** oOh!media shows the other side of the market: buyers are testing whether operational underperformance, contract risk and strategic synergies justify the price. [S3]

The AD takeaway is that **PE value creation and underwriting discipline matter as much as available capital**. A transaction may have multiple interested sponsors but still fail if the price does not support the investment thesis.

## 5. Infrastructure / institutional / sovereign capital

Digital infrastructure is increasingly a bridge between real estate, energy and technology. Centuria's potential industrial-to-data-centre conversion programme is a strong example: existing powered land can become an infrastructure platform, while funding could come through joint ventures, long-term leases or asset spin-offs. [S4]

The broader issue is **power availability and approvals**, not simply data-centre demand. Australia's renewable-power requirements create a new diligence dimension for investors underwriting AI infrastructure. [S5]

## 6. Cross-border capital

Cross-border capital remains prominent:

- Korea → Australia/US defence capability via Hanwha/Austal. [S1]
- Potential Canada/PE and international capital remains visible in other Australian competitive processes.
- Global capital is also shaping Australian resources markets: Glencore is seeking deeper engagement with Australia's institutional capital pool through its ASX listing. [S6]

The pattern to watch is **foreign strategic capital buying Australian capability, assets or platforms that provide access to a broader global market**.

## 7. Sector signals

### Resources / critical minerals
Glencore's ASX strategy is a capital-markets development with potential M&A implications. The company is targeting substantial copper growth and sees Australia as a strategically important investor base. [S6]

### TMT / technology / digital infrastructure
Data-centre demand is increasingly pulling property and energy capital into the technology infrastructure ecosystem. [S4][S5]

### Healthcare
Agilex and the wider clinical-trials ecosystem show continuing PE interest in specialised healthcare infrastructure and services. [S2]

### Defence / industrials
Austal demonstrates how Australian industrial assets can become components of global strategic supply chains. [S1]

## 8. Regulatory / ACCC / FIRB

Today's strongest regulatory signal is the **renewable-power requirement for new data centres**. This can affect project economics, development sequencing, power procurement and investment approvals. [S5]

For M&A generally, the new Australian merger-control regime remains a critical transaction-planning consideration. The agent should continue to monitor ACCC decisions, Phase 2 matters and threshold developments daily.

## 10. Deloitte AD lens — what I should know today

1. **Carve-outs are not just transaction mechanics.** Ask: *What does the buyer need to operate independently on Day 1, and which technology/data dependencies could destroy value?*
2. **PE processes are increasingly about underwriting the value-creation plan.** Ask: *What operational change makes the sponsor's price defensible?*
3. **Data centres are becoming an integrated M&A theme across property, power and technology.** Ask: *Who owns the powered land, who controls the energy, and who can execute the infrastructure programme?*
4. **Cross-border industrial M&A is increasingly strategic.** Ask: *Is the buyer acquiring an asset, or acquiring a position in a global capability/supply chain?*
5. **Regulation belongs in the transaction thesis early.** Ask: *Could approvals, energy policy or competition rules change the deal timetable or value case?*
